     1→     1→Complete Product Documentation: Adaptive Forex & Binary Options Learning Platform
     2→     2→Product blueprint · Version 1.0 · 15 October 2026 · Planning document
     3→     3→Our goal is to build more than a trading education website. We are planning an adaptive, self-evolving trading academy that teaches users from absolute zero, measures their understanding, guides their practical training, and continuously introduces new educational experiences as they progress.
     4→     4→The platform should be able to grow with the knowledge you add as its administrator. A beginner might start by learning what a currency pair is. Months later, the same learner could be studying risk management, strategy testing, trading psychology and advanced market analysis, with a dashboard tailored to their demonstrated skills.
     5→     5→This document defines the product vision, user experience, learning system, simulation engine, backend architecture, database, security, administration, development roadmap and the gaps we need to resolve before building.

    28→    28→1. Product vision and objectives
    29→    29→1.1 Vision
    30→    30→To create an accessible, practical, continuously evolving financial education platform where anyone can learn trading concepts from the beginning and develop their understanding through guided practice rather than relying on theory alone.
    31→    31→1.2 Mission
    32→    32→- Make trading education understandable to complete beginners.
    33→    33→- Connect every important concept to an exercise or simulation.
    34→    34→- Personalise the learning journey based on demonstrated knowledge.
    35→    35→- Teach risk awareness, probability and disciplined decision-making.
    36→    36→- Allow administrators to continually improve and expand the curriculum.
    37→    37→- Help learners measure their educational progress without misleading them about the likelihood of real-world trading success.
    38→    38→1.3 Product principles
    39→    39→1. Education before execution: users learn the relevant concept before attempting complex simulated trades.
    40→    40→2. Demonstrated mastery over time spent: completing a video does not automatically prove understanding.
    41→    41→3. Progress without artificial pressure: streaks, achievements and missions should encourage learning without creating gambling-like behaviour.
    42→    42→4. Simulation before any real-money consideration: the initial product is strictly educational and uses virtual funds.
    43→    43→5. Content that can evolve: administrators can publish new lessons without redeploying the whole application.
    44→    44→6. Transparent assessments: learners can understand why they passed, failed or received a recommendation.
    45→    45→7. Safety and honesty: no guaranteed-profit claims, fabricated performance results or misleading AI trading predictions.

   138→   138→4. The adaptive learning system
   139→   139→This is the central feature that differentiates the platform from a conventional course website.
   140→   140→4.1 Curriculum hierarchy
   141→   141→Every learning path follows a structured hierarchy:
   142→   142→Learning path
   143→   143→Example: Forex Fundamentals
   144→   144→
   145→   145→Course
   146→   146→Example: Understanding Currency Markets
   147→   147→
   148→   148→Chapter
   149→   149→Example: Currency Pairs
   150→   150→
   151→   151→Episode / lesson
   152→   152→Example: Understanding EUR/USD
   153→   153→
   154→   154→Learn
   155→   155→
   156→   156→
   157→   157→Assess
   158→   158→
   159→   159→
   160→   160→Practise
   161→   161→
   162→   162→
   163→   163→
   164→   164→
   165→   165→Each episode should have a stable identifier, content version, learning objectives, estimated duration, required skills, prerequisites, activities, assessment rules and publication status.
   166→   166→An episode may include text, illustrations, video, audio narration, an interactive chart, a quiz or a practical exercise. Administrators can combine these elements as appropriate.
   167→   167→4.2 How automatic progression works
   168→   168→The platform should use a deterministic progression engine with configurable rules.
   169→   169→1. Load the learner's curriculum and current progress.
   170→   170→2. Identify the skills needed for the next episode.
   171→   171→3. Check whether prerequisite skills have sufficient evidence of mastery.
   172→   172→4. Select the next appropriate activity.
   173→   173→5. Record the learner's answers, practical performance and mistakes.
   174→   174→6. Update the learner's skill records.
   175→   175→7. Advance, recommend revision or assign remedial practice.
   176→   176→8. Update the dashboard and record why the recommendation was made.
   177→   177→A simplified mastery rule might require at least 80% on a quiz and a pass on a practical exercise. That is an initial example; individual skills may require stricter or different assessments.
   178→   178→The platform should not treat these percentages as universal measures of competence. The administrator should configure appropriate criteria for each skill.
   179→   179→Skill mastery states
   180→   180→State   Meaning
   181→   181→Not started     The learner has not studied this skill.
   182→   182→Learning        The learner has begun the relevant lessons.
   183→   183→Developing      The learner shows partial understanding.
   184→   184→Practising      The learner is applying the skill in exercises.
   185→   185→Demonstrated    The learner has met the defined assessment criteria.
   186→   186→Needs revision  New evidence suggests the learner needs further practice.
   187→   187→A demonstrated skill should not be erased simply because a learner makes one mistake later. The system should use multiple attempts, evidence quality and spaced revision to decide when a reassessment is appropriate.
   188→   188→4.3 Diagnostic assessment
   189→   189→Every new learner receives an orientation and an optional or required diagnostic assessment.
   190→   190→The diagnostic should evaluate knowledge in areas such as:
   191→   191→- Basic financial concepts.
   192→   192→- Currency pairs and exchange rates.
   193→   193→- Candlestick interpretation.
   194→   194→- Risk and probability.
   195→   195→- Order types and trading terminology.
   196→   196→- Familiarity with simulation tools.
   197→   197→The diagnostic must distinguish a guess from demonstrated knowledge where practical. A learner who fails the diagnostic receives foundational lessons rather than being locked out of the platform.
   198→   198→Advanced learners may skip selected introductory content after meeting the relevant assessment criteria.
   199→   199→4.4 Revision and memory
   200→   200→The platform should include a spaced-repetition system.
   201→   201→For example, after learning a concept, the user may receive a brief review the next day, another several days later, and another after a longer interval. The schedule should adapt to performance and the importance of the skill.
   202→   202→A learner who repeatedly forgets how to calculate a position size should receive a targeted refresher and a new exercise, not merely be sent back to an entire course.
   203→   203→4.5 The knowledge graph
   204→   204→Every skill should connect to related skills and learning resources.
   205→   205→For example:
   206→   206→- Understanding pips supports position-size calculations.
   207→   207→- Position sizing supports risk-per-trade calculations.
   208→   208→- Risk-per-trade calculations support stop-loss planning.
   209→   209→- Stop-loss planning supports the risk-management practical assessment.
   210→   210→- Passing the risk-management assessment unlocks selected advanced simulation exercises.
   211→   211→This dependency graph allows new lessons to integrate into the curriculum without creating a disconnected library of content.

   213→   213→5. The evolving dashboard and engagement system
   214→   214→The dashboard is not a static collection of cards. It is a personalised view of what the learner should know, practise and discover next.
   215→   215→5.1 Dashboard sections
   216→   216→The dashboard should contain:
   217→   217→1. Continue learning — returns the user to the last unfinished activity.
   218→   218→2. Your next milestone — shows the nearest meaningful learning objective.
   219→   219→3. Newly unlocked — displays modules made available by demonstrated skills.
   220→   220→4. Recommended for you — presents targeted lessons for knowledge gaps.
   221→   221→5. Practice challenge — offers a relevant exercise in the simulator.
   222→   222→6. Skill map — displays demonstrated, developing and unstarted skills.
   223→   223→7. Review queue — lists concepts due for revision.
   224→   224→8. Learning statistics — shows lessons completed, assessment results and practice consistency.
   225→   225→9. Announcements — introduces newly published content relevant to the learner.
   226→   226→10. Achievements — recognises educational milestones and disciplined practice.
   227→   227→The initial dashboard should display only a manageable selection of these features, with additional capabilities appearing as the learner progresses.
   228→   228→5.2 Automatic introduction of new content
   229→   229→When an administrator publishes a new module, the content service should:
   230→   230→- Validate its publication status and prerequisites.
   231→   231→- Associate it with relevant skills and learning paths.
   232→   232→- Identify eligible learners.
   233→   233→- Determine whether it should be a required chapter, optional lesson, revision activity or advanced module.
   234→   234→- Add it to eligible learners' recommendations.
   235→   235→- Record the recommendation and its reason.
   236→   236→- Avoid repeatedly showing the same announcement.
   237→   237→- Allow learners to dismiss optional recommendations without losing access to the content.
   238→   238→A new module should not silently alter a learner's historical completion records. If the curriculum changes materially, the platform should retain version history and explain any new requirements.
   239→   239→5.3 Healthy gamification
   240→   240→The platform may include experience points, levels, badges, optional streaks, learning maps and non-financial competitions.
   241→   241→Rewards should recognise:
   242→   242→- Completing a course.
   243→   243→- Explaining a concept correctly.
   244→   244→- Improving after a mistake.
   245→   245→- Completing a risk-management exercise.
   246→   246→- Maintaining a consistent revision routine.
   247→   247→- Completing a historical simulation and reviewing its limitations.
   248→   248→Rewards should not depend solely on simulated profit, number of trades or willingness to take risks.

   250→   250→6. Forex learning curriculum
   251→   251→The initial curriculum should cover the following domains. It is a proposed structure to populate and refine, not a claim that the listed material is exhaustive.
   252→   252→Stage   Subject Practical application
   253→   253→1       Financial-market foundations    Explain trading, markets and risk
   254→   254→2       Forex terminology       Identify currency pairs, base and quote currencies
   255→   255→3       Exchange rates, pips and spreads        Calculate price changes and transaction costs
   256→   256→4       Candlesticks and timeframes     Interpret candles on sample charts
   257→   257→5       Trends and market structure     Annotate historical charts
   258→   258→6       Support and resistance  Mark candidate price levels
   259→   259→7       Indicators and technical analysis       Compare indicators and understand limitations
   260→   260→8       Fundamental analysis    Interpret selected economic scenarios
   261→   261→9       Orders and execution    Practise market and pending orders
   262→   262→10      Risk management Calculate position size and potential loss
   263→   263→11      Trading plans and psychology    Document a decision before simulated entry
   264→   264→12      Strategy testing        Backtest a defined set of rules
   265→   265→13      Performance evaluation  Analyse drawdown, costs and sample size
   266→   266→14      Advanced research       Test robustness and understand uncertainty
   267→   267→6.1 Risk management deserves its own learning path
   268→   268→This should be a major module rather than a single lesson.
   269→   269→It should cover:
   270→   270→- Risk per trade.
   271→   271→- Position sizing.
   272→   272→- Stop-loss and take-profit mechanics.
   273→   273→- Reward-to-risk ratios.
   274→   274→- Spread, commission and slippage.
   275→   275→- Leverage and margin.
   276→   276→- Daily and overall drawdown.
   277→   277→- Correlated positions.
   278→   278→- Gaps and execution uncertainty.
   279→   279→- Losing streaks and emotional decisions.
   280→   280→- Why a high win rate does not guarantee profitability.
   281→   281→- Why historical performance does not guarantee future results.
   282→   282→The practical lab should ask learners to calculate hypothetical risks and explain their decisions before executing simulated trades.
   283→   283→6.2 Binary-options curriculum
   284→   284→This should remain a separate educational pathway, not be mixed indistinguishably with forex.
   285→   285→Suggested topics:
   286→   286→- Contract mechanics and expiry.
   287→   287→- Fixed payouts and loss of stake.
   288→   288→- Break-even win-rate calculations.
   289→   289→- Expected value and probability.
   290→   290→- Short-term price uncertainty.
   291→   291→- Misleading claims and common scams.
   292→   292→- Jurisdictional restrictions and consumer protections.
   293→   293→- Differences between educational simulations and actual provider contracts.
   294→   294→The initial version should not accept deposits, place real trades or connect users to real-money binary-options execution.
   295→   295→
   296→   296→7. Trading simulation requirements
   297→   297→7.1 Historical market replay
   298→   298→The initial simulator should use licensed historical market data. Users should be able to pause, resume, advance and rewind within supported replay sessions.
   299→   299→The interface should support:
   300→   300→- Candlestick charts.
   301→   301→- Instrument selection.
   302→   302→- Timeframe selection.
   303→   303→- Historical replay speed.
   304→   304→- Drawing tools and selected indicators.
   305→   305→- Entry and exit markers.
   306→   306→- Virtual account balance and equity.
   307→   307→- Open-position details.
   308→   308→- Simulated order history.
   309→   309→- Stop-loss and take-profit settings.
   310→   310→- Trade notes and post-trade review.
   311→   311→The replay engine must prevent look-ahead bias: learners should not see future prices when making decisions. Historical data must be correctly ordered, and the system must clearly label synthetic, delayed or incomplete data.
   312→   312→7.2 Forex virtual account
   313→   313→Each learner receives an isolated virtual account with configurable starting funds.
   314→   314→The simulator must support a consistent set of accounting rules covering:
   315→   315→- Available balance.
   316→   316→- Equity.
   317→   317→- Unrealised and realised profit or loss.
   318→   318→- Position size and contract specifications.
   319→   319→- Spread and commission.
   320→   320→- Margin and leverage assumptions.
   321→   321→- Stop-loss and take-profit execution.
   322→   322→- Swap or financing costs if included in the chosen model.
   323→   323→- Slippage and gaps where supported.
   324→   324→- Account limits and reset behaviour.
   325→   325→Virtual money must be clearly distinguished from real money. Users should not be able to transfer simulated balances into financial accounts or mistake virtual performance for verified real-world results.
   326→   326→7.3 Example position-sizing calculation
   327→   327→For a simple illustrative trade with a USD-denominated account:
   328→   328→- Virtual account balance: $10,000.
   329→   329→- Hypothetical risk budget: 1%, or $100.
   330→   330→- Stop distance: 20 pips.
   331→   331→- Assumed pip value: $10 per pip per standard lot.
   332→   332→The simplified position size is:
   333→   333→\[
   334→   334→\text{Lots}=\frac{\$100}{20\times \$10}=0.5
   335→   335→\]
   336→   336→
   337→   337→This example ignores transaction costs and assumes the stated pip value applies. The actual simulator must derive pip value from the instrument, position size and account currency, and account for fees and execution assumptions.
   338→   338→7.4 Binary-options scenario simulator
   339→   339→The binary simulator should use recorded prices and hypothetical contracts. Learners select a scenario, a hypothetical stake and an expiry interval, then evaluate the outcome under explicitly documented rules.
   340→   340→It should display payout, loss, break-even win rate and expected value. It should not imply that a successful simulated prediction establishes a repeatable trading edge.
   341→   341→7.5 Post-trade analysis
   342→   342→Every completed practice trade should be reviewable.
   343→   343→The system can evaluate whether the learner followed their stated plan, understood their risk, applied the intended order rules and documented the result.
   344→   344→It must separate process quality from financial outcome. A trade that makes a virtual profit after an undisciplined decision should not automatically be labelled a good trade.
   345→   345→
   346→   346→8. AI learning coach
   347→   347→The AI coach should support the learning engine rather than replace it.
   348→   348→8.1 Core capabilities
   349→   349→- Explain trading concepts in beginner-friendly language.
   350→   350→- Offer alternative explanations when a learner is confused.
   351→   351→- Generate practice questions from approved educational material.
   352→   352→- Explain why an answer is incorrect.
   353→   353→- Summarise the learner's recent practice performance.
   354→   354→- Recommend lessons based on recorded knowledge gaps.
   355→   355→- Help learners understand charts and simulated-trade outcomes.
   356→   356→- Generate draft content for administrator review.
   357→   357→- Answer questions using the platform's approved knowledge library.
   358→   358→8.2 How it should work
   359→   359→The AI coach receives the learner's relevant progress, the current lesson and approved reference material. It returns an explanation, an optional exercise and references to relevant lessons.
   360→   360→The system should not expose unnecessary personal data to an AI provider. It should also distinguish verified educational facts from generated explanations and disclose when an answer is uncertain.
   361→   361→AI-generated lessons, quizzes and assessment answers should not be automatically published without suitable review.
   362→   362→8.3 What the AI coach must not do
   363→   363→- Promise that a user will become profitable.
   364→   364→- Present speculative predictions as facts.
   365→   365→- Encourage a learner to recover losses by increasing risk.
   366→   366→- Make unlocking decisions solely from conversational impressions.
   367→   367→- Invent market prices or claim a simulated result came from a live market.
   368→   368→- Treat a few successful simulated trades as proof of skill.
   369→   369→Mastery decisions should be made by the assessment and progression services using explicit, testable rules.

   465→   465→The final schema will require additional details and constraints, but these entities provide the starting point.
   466→   466→10.2 Important relationships
   467→   467→- One learning path contains many courses or curriculum units.
   468→   468→- One course contains chapters; chapters contain episodes.
   469→   469→- One episode can teach multiple skills.
   470→   470→- One skill can depend on several prerequisite skills.
   471→   471→- One user has progress records for many episodes and skills.
   472→   472→- One assessment has many attempts by many learners.
   473→   473→- One learner has isolated simulation accounts.
   474→   474→- One simulation account has orders, positions and executions.
   475→   475→- One replay session references a specific dataset and replay configuration.
   476→   476→- One content version records the published state of an episode at a particular time.
   477→   477→Use stable IDs and database constraints. Do not use a lesson title as its identifier, because administrators may rename lessons.
   478→   478→10.3 Data integrity rules
   479→   479→- Store timestamps in a consistent timezone, normally UTC.
   480→   480→- Record the source, timeframe and version of market data.
   481→   481→- Use appropriate decimal precision for financial calculations.
   482→   482→- Avoid floating-point arithmetic for authoritative balances and money calculations; use fixed-point or exact decimal arithmetic.
   483→   483→- Keep an immutable history of executed simulation events.
   484→   484→- Use database transactions for operations that change balances or positions.
   485→   485→- Make order submission idempotent so retries cannot accidentally execute the same request twice.
   486→   486→- Preserve published content versions and assessment history.
   487→   487→- Prevent users from changing their own roles, assessment results or authoritative balances through client requests.
   488→   488→10.4 Example: how publishing new content works
   489→   489→An administrator publishes a risk-management course.
   490→   490→The database stores its course, chapters, episodes, learning objectives, prerequisites and version. The progression service checks the skill graph for learners who may benefit. It creates recommendations for eligible learners and prerequisite reminders for those who are not yet ready.
   491→   491→When the learner opens the dashboard, the frontend requests their current recommendations and displays the relevant new module. The system does not need to rewrite individual learner records just because a new course exists.
   492→   492→This design makes curriculum expansion practical and auditable.

   573→   573→13. Security, privacy and financial boundaries
   574→   574→Security should be built into the architecture from the beginning.
   575→   575→13.1 Account security
   576→   576→- Use a maintained authentication system.
   577→   577→- Require secure session handling and account recovery.
   578→   578→- Protect administrative accounts with multifactor authentication where supported.
   579→   579→- Enforce roles and permissions on the server.
   580→   580→- Rate-limit login and password-reset endpoints.
   581→   581→- Log sensitive administrative changes.
   582→   582→- Provide account deletion and appropriate data export functionality.
   583→   583→13.2 Database security
   584→   584→Supabase Row Level Security should protect user-owned records. Learners should only read and modify the records their permissions allow.
   585→   585→Administrative access should use narrowly scoped policies and server-side authorisation. Service-role keys must never be included in frontend bundles.
   586→   586→Simulation balances and mastery results must not be writable through unrestricted client requests.
   587→   587→13.3 Financial and regulatory scope
   588→   588→The first release is an educational platform, not a broker, investment adviser or real-money execution system.
   589→   589→Before introducing real-money trading, broker integrations, referrals to trading providers or paid binary-options functionality, obtain jurisdiction-specific legal advice. Binary-options products are prohibited or restricted in some jurisdictions, and their regulatory treatment depends on the product, provider and location.
   590→   590→We should also review privacy requirements, consumer-protection obligations, advertising claims, age policies, intellectual-property licences and the terms governing market-data use. If serving users in Kenya or other jurisdictions, these matters need to be assessed for the intended operating model.
   591→   591→A disclaimer alone does not make a regulated activity lawful.

   803→   803→18. My final recommendation
   804→   804→There are three things I would prioritise above everything else.
   805→   805→First: build the learning engine before building a massive course library. A smaller collection of well-designed lessons that automatically adapts to a learner is more valuable than hundreds of disconnected articles.
   806→   806→Second: make the Knowledge Studio powerful enough to grow the platform. Adding a new lesson should be a content-management task, not a software-development task. You should be able to create a course, attach its learning objectives, define prerequisites, publish it, and have the progression engine distribute it appropriately.
   807→   807→Third: make the simulator trustworthy. Its calculations, market-data assumptions, execution rules and historical replay behaviour must be tested. The simulator is where learners turn their knowledge into practice, so incorrect results would undermine the entire product.
   808→   808→The defining feature should be this:
   809→   809→Every lesson builds a skill. Every assessment provides evidence. Every skill influences what comes next. Every new piece of knowledge can create a new learning experience.
   810→   810→
   811→   811→That is the foundation of a genuinely progressive trading academy.
   812→   812→The documentation above is our product blueprint, not proof that the application has already been built. Before coding, the next deliverable should be a definitive product requirements document, followed by the database schema, API specification, curriculum rules and a prioritised development backlog. We should then implement and test the platform one phase at a time
